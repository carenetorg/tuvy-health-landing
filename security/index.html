Data & Security

Last updated: 19 October 2026
Questions or disclosures: admin@tuvy.health

Tuvy holds health information about people who are often not the person operating the app. That asymmetry is the reason for most of what follows.

Encryption

  • At rest. Identifying details and the most sensitive health fields — names, dates of birth, addresses, allergies, reports and visit notes — are encrypted with AES-256-GCM in our application layer before they reach the database, so the stored value is ciphertext rather than readable text. Not every field is: vital-sign readings, for example, rely on Google Cloud's own encryption at rest.
  • In transit. All traffic between the apps and our services runs over TLS.
  • Keys are held in Google Secret Manager and loaded at runtime. They are not in our source code.

Who can see what

  • Every protected endpoint enforces role-based access control — family member, care professional, and operations are separate roles with separate reach.
  • Within a family, each member has a per-member visibility setting. Fields a member has not been granted are removed before the data leaves our servers, not hidden in the app.
  • A newly invited family member sees only a limited set by default — visit times and records, daily check-ins, and where a visit is happening — and not vitals, medications, documents or reports until the account owner grants them. The family's responsible party sees everything.

The visit record

Each visit builds a SHA-256 hash chain: every recorded event includes the hash of the one before it, so an event cannot be altered or removed after the fact without breaking the chain. The family receives the resulting certificate.

Where your data lives

Our services run on Google Cloud in the United States (Iowa). Health data about care recipients in India is therefore stored and processed in the US. This is stated plainly in our Privacy Policy, and it is a deliberate disclosure rather than a detail.

Operational practice

  • Before a care professional can be matched, Operations must record an in-person ID check, mark her application verified and switch her on for matching. A nurse's council registration must be checked, and a licence past its expiry date takes a professional out of the pool. We ask every professional for a police clearance certificate and review it, but today a missing certificate does not by itself stop a match.
  • Secrets live in Secret Manager, so replacing one does not mean changing code.

What we do not claim

Security pages usually list only strengths. These are the things we are asked about that we cannot yet answer with a yes:

  • We are not SOC 2 certified. We maintain a control evidence map internally, which is preparation for an audit — not the same as having passed one.
  • Face matching has no liveness check. Caregiver and patient each take a face scan at the door; we compare it, on our own servers, with the photograph that person enrolled and seal the result into the visit record. The scan itself is not kept. We do not yet perform liveness detection, so a printed photograph could pass. We will say so here when that changes.
  • We have not completed an independent penetration test. The scope is defined; the test has not been carried out.
  • Council registration is checked by people, not by a live registry feed. There is no reliable public API for the Indian Nursing Council register, so our Operations team checks the registration number of each caregiver whose role has one before approving her, and records who approved it.

If any of this matters to a decision you are making, ask us directly and we will tell you where things actually stand.

Reporting a vulnerability

Email admin@tuvy.health with enough detail to reproduce the issue. We will acknowledge it, and we will not pursue anyone who reports a genuine finding in good faith and does not access or alter other people's data while investigating.
